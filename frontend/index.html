Map Elements

Radiance glow: Soft headlight-style circles; color = % vs 2024 (presets) or first selected year (manual), grey = baseline year, yellow/green/red = stable/up/down (±40%)

Analysis radius (dashed ring): A fixed-distance circle around the geocoded city center—not an official city, district, or port boundary.

Percentage: Change vs the first year (same month). Helps avoid seasonal effects.

Gaps / NULL months: Months with very low cloud-free coverage are treated as missing (NULL) rather than 0.

What area does each value represent?

Not a statutory map. Outlines on this map are approximate analysis zones for orientation only. They do not follow legal city limits, district boundaries, port authorities, or cadastral parcels.

How a place is located: The app geocodes the name you enter (via OpenStreetMap) to a single center point (latitude/longitude).

How the radius is chosen: Each place uses a preset radius in kilometres (e.g. larger metros ~15–30 km, smaller hubs ~8–12 km). The dashed blue ring is a simple circle around that point—it is not traced from an administrative shapefile.

How VIIRS feeds the graph: For each month, VIIRS DNB pixels inside a square box (±radius around the center) are averaged with equal weight, then bias-corrected (Elvidge et al., 2021). Satellite ground spacing is ~750 m, so one monthly point summarizes a broad neighbourhood, not a pixel-perfect footprint.

What to expect: Use this tool for trends over time and broad comparisons between places analyzed the same way—not for exact urban extent, zoning, or official statistics. Coastal, linear, or irregular cities may include water, farmland, or suburbs in the average.
